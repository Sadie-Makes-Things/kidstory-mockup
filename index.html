SESSION 5 · TELL THE STORY
SUNBEAM
Trainer Note

Sensory & Vocal Facilitation

Everyone retains more when they do something rather than just hear it. Instead of lecturing on the five senses, have groups blind-test an object to guess how it relates to the story of Peter walking on the water.

  • Vocal Variety. Demonstrate a monotone voice versus an expressive voice. Ask the participants: which one makes you want to lean in, and which one makes you want to look at your phone?
  • The Power of the Pause. Trainers often rush to fill silence. Model an 8-second pause after a dramatic moment in the story. This silence is where the Holy Spirit works on the heart.
  • Believability over Performance. Participants don't need to be professional actors. If they believe the story is true and live it out with the children, the audience will follow.
Core Principle

God's Style of Storytelling

We aim to tell stories in a way that is Memorable, Unexpected, and Multi-Sensory. God used these same principles throughout Scripture to ensure His message was never forgotten. Think about how you might use your location, lighting, seating, or even the senses themselves to surprise the audience and raise curiosity.

SESSION 5 · ABOVE ALL
SUNBEAM

Live It Out

Your passion and presentation for the story is not what the children will remember most. Our individual actions and attitudes often help children understand who Jesus is more than our spoken words.

We cared so much for you that we were pleased to share with you not only the Gospel of God, but also our own lives.
1 THESSALONIANS 2:8

Preach the Gospel at all times, and words are helpful too.

Reference
  • Place. Sitting in small groups (Jesus Feeds the Multitudes), or seated like they're in a boat (Jesus Walks on the Water).
  • Visual. Costumes or simple objects like a piece of fabric.
  • Touch. Passing an object, or a light splash of water.
  • Hear. Rubbing hands for rain, or vocal sound effects.
  • Smell / Taste. Incense, fish, or even a piece of bread.

SESSION 5 · DELIVERY IN PRACTICE
SUNBEAM
Trainer Note

The Storyteller's Toolbox

Your voice and body are the primary instruments for discipling the children's imagination.

  • Movement. Don't stay glued to your chair. Move through the locations of the story to help the children visualize the journey.
  • Voice. Vary your Pitch to represent different characters or emotions. Control your Pace, slowing down for mystery and speeding up for action. Utilize the Pause to let a specific predicament or truth sink from the head to the heart.
  • Facial Expressions. Your expressions make the story believable. If your face doesn't reflect the emotion of the text, the children will sense a disconnect.
Peer Activity

The Expression Challenge. Turn to a partner. Without using words, use your face to portray 3 emotions from the Expressions Sheet below (e.g., frightened, amazed, frustrated). Your partner must guess the emotion and identify a moment in the Jesus and Peter story where a character felt that way.

SESSION 5 · AUDIENCE PARTICIPATION & LAB ACTIVITY
SUNBEAM
Peer Activity

Audience Participation

Get the children into the story, not just in front of it.

  • Reoccurring Phrases. Let the group join in on repeated words.
  • Hand & Body Motions. Assign motions for specific actions (e.g., rowing a boat, blowing wind).
  • Human Slide Show. Have children freeze in poses representing scenes as you tell them.
Lab Activity

Into the Lab: The Sensory Tell

Get back into your small groups. Practice telling the story of Jesus and Peter Walking on the Water seeking to utilize all of the senses you can.

  • Sound Effect Chief. One person is responsible for the wind and waves.
  • Prop Master. Use one random object (paper, a scarf, a cup, a stick, a little bit of water) to represent something in the story.
  • The Tell. The storyteller must incorporate at least one Pause and one Vocal Shift (Pitch or Pace).
